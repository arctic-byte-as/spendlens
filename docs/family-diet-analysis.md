## Dietary Assessment

### What to cut out

**1. Bulk candy / smågodt — 12,272 NOK (7.4%)**
`Smågodt pr kg` was purchased **35 times for 5,857 NOK** — nearly 500 NOK/month on bulk pick-n-mix. Pure added sugar, zero nutrition.
- Target: ≤500 NOK/year

**2. Processed meat — 9,497 NOK (5.7%)**
Bacon (26x Stjernebacon), salami, chorizo, knackwurst, leverpostei weekly. WHO classifies processed meat as a **Group 1 carcinogen**. NNR recommends max 50g/week for a family.
- Biggest culprits: Stjernebacon (26x), Salami Napoli (6x), Chorizoboller (6x), Knacker (7x)

**3. Sweet drinks — 2,754 NOK**
Cola Zero is calorie-free but maintains sugar cravings and may affect gut microbiome. Solo Super (sugared) and julebrus are worse. Sjokoladedrikk is liquid sugar.
- Switch to: Farris, water, homemade infused water

**4. Chips & savoury snacks — 2,550 NOK**
Superchips (17x) and Kims Potetgull (17x) are a consistent weekly pattern — ultra-processed, high salt, low nutrition. Pommes frites (14x, 545 NOK) also notable.

### What to adapt / moderate

**5. Cheese — 13,546 NOK (8.2%)**
More cheese spend than fish. Selbu Blå (blue cheese) bought **39 times** for 1,509 NOK. Norvegia 1kg blocks 15 times. High in saturated fat and salt at this volume.
- Keep: mozzarella, feta, cottage cheese
- Reduce: Norvegia blocks, Selbu Blå frequency

**6. Red meat — 13,044 NOK (7.9%)**
Minced beef/pork, pork strips, ribeye, and whole cuts regularly. NNR recommends max 350–500g cooked red meat per week. Combined with processed meat, weekly total is likely above guidelines.
- Replace 2 red meat dinners/week with fish or legumes

**7. Alcohol — 9,099 NOK (5.5%)**
Mostly lite/low-alcohol variants (Ringnes Lite, Aass Lite), but still 5.5% of food budget. NNR 2023 revised to "no safe level" of alcohol.

**8. Refined bread & tortillas — 5,848 NOK**
Tortillas bought **59 times** — the main gluten-free wrap option. Fine occasionally but highly refined. Yoghurtbrød (90x) is the most-bought bread — decent but low-fibre compared to real whole grain options.

**9. High-sugar condiments**
BBQ sauce (10x, 447 NOK) contains ~30% sugar. Ketchup (506 NOK). Fine for occasional grilling, but at this frequency it contributes significantly to added sugar intake.

### What to eat more of

**10. Fish — only 2.8%, target 2–3 meals/week**
The biggest nutritional gap. Some hyse (19x) and occasional laks, but nowhere near NNR recommendations. Fish provides omega-3, iodine, selenium, and vitamin D.
- Add: canned mackerel (cheap), frozen cod, more salmon portions
- Target: 600–900g fish/week for a family

**11. Legumes — 3.2%**
White beans (19x) and chickpeas (18x) are present — good start. But lentils nearly absent. Legumes are cheap, filling, high in fibre and protein, and strongly linked to reduced chronic disease risk.
- Add: lentil soup or bean dish 2x/week

**12. More variety in vegetables**
Heavy on cucumber (49x), carrots (32x), salad mix (41x). Add more calorie-dense veg: sweet potato (some already), squash, beets, more broccoli.
